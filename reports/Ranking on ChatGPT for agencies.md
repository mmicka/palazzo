# Win ChatGPT's Shortlist With Third-Party Proof

As of October 2026, ChatGPT recommends agencies the way people do at a dinner party: it repeats the names it hears from others. When someone asks it for an agency, ChatGPT usually runs several web searches. It reads third-party "best agencies" lists, directory pages such as Clutch, and review sites, then names the agencies that keep coming up. The agency's own website mostly serves to confirm details once the agency is already on the shortlist. **Digital Trek appears on none of the lists ChatGPT is likely to read for Vancouver, trades or clinic questions.** It also has no Clutch reviews we could verify, lists three different home cities across the web, and shares a name with a larger Vancouver firm, Trek Marketing. The fixes that matter most are plain ones. Let ChatGPT's crawler read the site, make every listing say the same thing, collect verified Clutch reviews, get onto niche local lists, and earn mentions through original data, the founder's LinkedIn and YouTube. Popular shortcuts such as llms.txt files, FAQ schema and "AI-only" pages show no measurable effect, and hidden-text tricks are now classed as attacks. Measure progress as the share of repeated ChatGPT runs that mention you, plus the leads who say ChatGPT sent them. Don't track a "rank," because answer lists almost never repeat. Treat every figure in this report with caution. Nearly all studies come from vendors that sell AI-visibility tools, most show correlation rather than cause, and every number came from search-result summaries rather than full-page reads, so check figures against the original before repeating them to clients.

## ChatGPT builds its agency shortlist from other people's lists

**The stakes are real but mostly invisible.** ChatGPT had **900 million weekly users** in February 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/chatgpt-reaches-900m-weekly-active-users)). In a G2 survey, 51% of B2B software buyers said they now start research in a chatbot more often than in a search engine ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report)). A BrightLocal survey, reported secondhand, found 45% of US consumers had used AI tools to find local businesses in the past year ([Cloutly](https://cloutly.com/blog/ai-search-statistics/)). Neither survey covers people hiring agencies, G2 makes money from review listings, and both are US data. Read them as a sign of direction, not of size.

**Two paths: memory or live search.** ChatGPT answers either from what it learned in training or by searching the web in real time. OpenAI says only that it searches "automatically" when a question would benefit from current information ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search)). Independent tests agree that "best X" questions trigger a search far more often than general questions: **73% versus 10%** in a 400-query test ([AI+Automation](https://aiplusautomation.com/blog/when-does-chatgpt-search)), and 53.5% versus 18.7% in Nectiv's larger dataset ([Otterly](https://otterly.ai/blog/how-often-does-chatgpt-trigger-a-web-search/)). Still, one commercial prompt that was tracked repeatedly searched in only 31% of runs ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/do-not-force-search-prompt-tracking)). You therefore need a web footprint ChatGPT can find today and enough history that the model learned about you in training.

**Where ChatGPT searches keeps changing.** "ChatGPT uses Bing" is out of date. Microsoft says its Bing-powered Web IQ service supports ChatGPT ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/web-iq/overview)). Peec AI found OpenAI testing its own index, nicknamed "Labrador," alongside outside scraping providers, and only 1.5% of Labrador's results appeared in Bing's top 20 ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/chatgpt-built-its-own-search-index)). The Information reported in 2025 that OpenAI also used scraped Google results ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/openai-chatgpt-serpapi-google-search-results-461226)). OpenAI has not confirmed any of these sources by name, and Peec sells tracking tools. In practice, **only about 8% of pages ChatGPT cited ranked in Google's or Bing's top 10** for the same question in a 2025 study ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/)). Other studies disagree sharply on Bing: older Seer Interactive figures put the match with Bing's top results at 80-87%, while Grow and Convert found about 40% ([Frase](https://www.frase.io/blog/which-ai-engines-cite-which-sources)). The shift to OpenAI's own index likely weakens any Bing link. Either way, a #1 Google ranking neither guarantees a ChatGPT mention nor is required for one.

**One question becomes several searches.** ChatGPT rewrites the user's question into a handful of smaller web searches, a step the industry calls "fan-out." It often adds "best," "reviews" and the current year ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/patterns-we-see-in-chatgpt-query-fanouts)). In September 2026, "2026" appeared in 22.9% of these searches and "price" in 8.9% ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt/)).

*Example:* a roofing contractor in Burnaby asks, "Who's the best marketing agency for roofers near Vancouver?" ChatGPT might search "best roofing marketing agencies Vancouver 2026" and "roofing marketing agency Vancouver reviews." It could land on Webtonic's 2026 list of Vancouver roofing marketing agencies ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/best-roofing-marketing-agencies-vancouver)) and Clutch's Vancouver rankings ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/ca/agencies/digital-marketing/vancouver)), then name the firms that appear on both. Digital Trek is on neither, so it doesn't get named.

**Lists decide who gets considered, and your site confirms the details.** "Best X" list articles made up **43.8% of 26,283 pages ChatGPT cited** across 750 recommendation prompts, some of them about agencies. Brands ranked higher on those lists showed up in answers more often ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/)). About a third of the cited lists sat on low-authority sites, so a list on a small local blog can count. After a 2026 model update, lists' share of citations roughly halved, but the number of lists cited per answer stayed flat because answers cited more sources overall ([SEO Handbook](https://seohandbook.co.uk/seo-news-updates/chatgpt-fan-out-query-shift-listicles/)). That finding comes from a social-media write-up that still needs confirming.

A smaller October 2026 test pushes back on the importance of lists. Across 31 "best agency for X" questions, **57% of ChatGPT's citations pointed to the named agencies' own websites**, 22% to lists and 1% to Reddit ([Onely](https://www.onely.com/blog/how-chatgpt-decides-which-brands-to-recommend/)). Onely is itself an agency and the sample is small. Still, both results fit one explanation: third-party lists put you on the shortlist, and ChatGPT then visits the shortlisted sites to check services, location and price.

**Answers change with each user and each run.** ChatGPT estimates a user's rough location from their internet connection and can rewrite searches using memories it has saved about them ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search)). In a study of 2,961 runs of 12 prompts, **the odds of getting the same list of brands twice were under 1 in 100** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-recommendation-lists-rarely-repeat-study-468076)). The study was co-run by Gumshoe, which sells tracking tools, and it was not peer reviewed. Smaller, tighter categories gave more stable answers. That favours a question like "roofing marketing agency in Vancouver" over "best digital agency in Canada."

**You can't buy your way in, and local answers draw on Yelp.** OpenAI says the ads it introduced in 2026 do not influence ChatGPT's answers ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/our-approach-to-advertising-and-expanding-access/)). OpenAI has published no ranking factors. One researcher examined ChatGPT's traffic, found no authority or trust score in it, and concluded that anyone selling "ChatGPT ranking factors" is guessing ([Suganthan](https://suganthan.com/ai-seo-research/chatgpt/)). For consumer "near me" questions, Yelp licensed 330 million reviews to OpenAI in July 2026 ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/07/23/yelp-reviews-chatgpt-geo-partnership)). One vendor's test of 2,880 prompts found Yelp data behind about 96% of local business cards ([Surface Local](https://www.surfacelocal.com/blog/how-chatgpt-finds-local-businesses)). That matters more for your clients' customers than for Digital Trek itself.

## Off-site proof outweighs anything you publish about yourself

The tables in this section and the next use six plain grades:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | OpenAI's own documentation says so. |
| **Strong signal** | Several large studies agree. They are run by vendors and show that two things go together, not that one causes the other. |
| **Some evidence** | The studies are small, stand alone or conflict with each other. |
| **Weak** | Evidence is thin, swings wildly over time, or mostly points the other way. |
| **Unproven** | No measured effect. |
| **Avoid** | No effect, or a real risk. |

| Off-site signal | Grade | What the evidence shows | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Being named on third-party "best agencies in [city or niche]" lists | Strong signal | Lists were 43.8% of pages ChatGPT cited, and brands ranked higher on them were mentioned more ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/)). Ranking #1 on a widely cited list moved brands earlier in answers, with smaller gains after a few top spots ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/the-listicle-rank-effect-what-nearly-200-000-ai-responses-across-8-ai-engines-reveal-about-brand-visibility)). | Both vendors sell tracking tools. |
| A Clutch profile with verified reviews | Strong signal for agencies | Clutch was the #2 most-cited site in ChatGPT in September 2026 (25,864 citations), behind only Reddit ([Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/most-cited-domains-chatgpt/)). It was also the top directory in a 50-answer audit of agency questions ([SCALZ.AI](https://scalz.ai/blog/agency-directories-ai-citations/)). | Surfer's users are marketers, which inflates agency prompts. Another study found B2B review directories in only 6 of 500 answers ([SourceRank AI](https://sourcerankai.com/blog/do-g2-and-clutch-reviews-help-ai-citation/)). |
| Brand mentions across the web, especially on YouTube | Strong signal | Across 75,000 brands, YouTube mentions had the highest correlation with AI visibility, about 0.74 on a 0-to-1 scale ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations)). | The sample covered established sites only. |
| Earned media (articles other people write about you) | Strong signal | 84% of 25M+ links cited by ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini were earned media, and paid content was 0.3% ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/blog/what-is-ai-reading-may-2026)). A University of Toronto paper found AI search strongly favours earned media over brand-owned content ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08919)). | Muck Rack sells PR software, and the paper has not been peer reviewed. |
| The founder's LinkedIn activity | Some evidence | LinkedIn climbed from #11 to #5 among ChatGPT's cited sites between November 2025 and February 2026 ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/linkedin-is-the-most-cited-domain-for-professional-queries-in-ai-search)). 59% of those citations came from personal profiles, not company pages ([The State of Brand](https://www.thestateofbrand.com/news/linkedin-top-ai-citation-for-b2b)). | Short time window, vendor data. |
| Google reviews and Google Business Profile | Some evidence (local questions) | In small local tests, answers were built from profile fields such as rating and review count ([LinkTech](https://linktechsolutions.io/insights/how-chatgpt-picks-local-businesses)). | The tests were small, and B2B agency questions were not studied. |
| Your own fair "best of" list | Some evidence, rising risk | When a new brand published such a list, it appeared in 66.4% of answers to a narrow question but only 15.8% for a broad one ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/)). Of six engines, ChatGPT cites self-ranking lists the least, about 4% ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/self-promotional-listicles-analysis-from-232k-citations)). | Google's AI Overviews cited such lists but left the author out 69% of the time ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-cite-self-serving-listicles-recommend-competitors-480573)). Sites relying on them lost 30-50% of Google visibility, though most were also using other risky tactics ([ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/self-promotional-listicles-google-rankings-2026/)). |
| Reddit | Weak and unstable | Reddit was 1% of citations in the 31-question agency test ([Onely](https://www.onely.com/blog/how-chatgpt-decides-which-brands-to-recommend/)). Its share of ChatGPT citations fell from 3.83% to 0.52% within days in August 2026 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/why-reddits-chatgpt-citation-drop-isnt-fully-explained/534572/)). | Tracking tools disagree sharply. |
| DesignRush, GoodFirms, Sortlist, UpCity | Weak | Only small audits exist, and some DesignRush placements are paid ([Lemon.io](https://lemon.io/blog/clutch-alternatives/)). | No large studies. |
| Wikidata, Crunchbase, matching name, address and phone everywhere | Unproven | Widely recommended, but no before-and-after result has been measured ([LLMrefs](https://llmrefs.com/blog/chatgpt-entities)). | Still worth doing to prevent mix-ups with similar names. |
| A Wikipedia article | Unproven and unrealistic | Wikipedia requires significant coverage in independent sources ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(organizations_and_companies))). | Press coverage has to come first. |
| Paid awards | Avoid | No study measures any effect ([Stanislav Peev](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/owned-review-sites-ai-visibility/)). | The claims come from people who sell the awards. |
| Backlinks on their own | Weak | Correlation was 0.22 for backlinks versus 0.66 for brand mentions ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/branded-web-mentions-visibility-ai-search/)). | 2025 data, and measured on Google's AI Overviews, not ChatGPT. |

The pattern points to an order of work: Clutch reviews first, then niche local lists, then a steady flow of mentions earned from others. Two warnings apply. First, no independent large study looks only at agency questions. No verified case study shows an agency raising its ChatGPT recommendations through off-site work, and Onely found no evidence that any agency AI engines recommend for "ChatGPT SEO" had ever gotten a client named ([Onely](https://www.onely.com/blog/best-chatgpt-seo-agencies/)). Second, the Ahrefs mention study looked at established brands, so the effect could be smaller for a one-office agency. There is one encouraging finding. Of the three AI platforms Ahrefs measured, ChatGPT had the weakest link to backlink strength, which gives small agencies a better shot here than in Google's AI Mode ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ahrefs-youtube-mentions-ai-visibility-brand-search)).

## On-site work is about access and clarity, not tricks

Your website has two jobs: let ChatGPT read it, and make it easy to confirm what you do, for whom, where and at what price. Rigorous tests find that clever rewriting aimed at AI rarely works, while plain relevance and strong search visibility do. In C-SEO Bench, a peer-reviewed benchmark from NeurIPS 2025, most AI-specific rewriting methods were "largely ineffective," and traditional SEO was "significantly more effective" ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=oTeixD3oZO)). A July 2026 review of 45 studies found no technique with a stable, lasting effect across platforms ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035)).

| On-site tactic | Grade | What the evidence shows | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allow OpenAI's search crawler, "OAI-SearchBot," in robots.txt (the public file that tells bots what they may read). Don't mark key pages "noindex." | Confirmed | Sites that block OAI-SearchBot "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). Training access ("GPTBot") is a separate setting. | Hosting or Cloudflare bot-blocking can block the crawler without you knowing ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001243-advertiser-guidance-for-allowing-openai-web-crawlers)). |
| Key facts written in the page's text, not loaded later by scripts | Strong signal | OpenAI's crawlers did not run JavaScript in tests ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)). | The test dates from December 2024, Vercel sells hosting, and one unverified September 2026 report suggests this may be changing. |
| One focused page per service, niche or city you truly serve | Strong signal | Relevance is the most consistent lever in academic tests ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=oTeixD3oZO)). Across 815,000 question-page pairs, focused pages beat long "ultimate guides" ([Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/shorter-focused-content-wins-in-chatgpt)). | One large vendor study found longer pages were cited more ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/new-data-top-factors-influencing-chatgpt-citations/561954/)). |
| Clear prices or price ranges | Some evidence | ChatGPT's own searches often add "price" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt/)). | Correlation only. |
| Content you genuinely update, with visible dates | Some evidence | ChatGPT favoured recent content more than any other assistant in a 17-million-page study ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/)). | The average cited page was still about 2.9 years old, and changing dates without real updates is risky. |
| Answer-first writing: who, what and where in the opening lines | Some evidence | 44.2% of ChatGPT citations came from the first 30% of a page ([ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/chatgpt-citations-study-44-percent-first-third-content/)). | Reported secondhand, and the sample sizes conflict. |
| Statistics, quotes and original data | Some evidence | A 2024 academic paper reported gains of up to about 40%, but only for pages ChatGPT had already found. Later tests mostly failed to repeat the result ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035)). | Helps how much of your page gets used, not whether you're found. |
| Content that appears quickly on screen | Some evidence | Pages that showed first content in under 0.4 seconds averaged about 6.7 citations, versus about 2.1 for slow pages ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/new-data-top-factors-influencing-chatgpt-citations/561954/)). | Correlation only. |
| Schema (behind-the-scenes code that labels facts such as your address) | Unproven | 1,885 pages that added schema saw no significant gain in ChatGPT citations ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)). | Cheap, and it helps separate you from firms with similar names. |
| Bing Webmaster Tools, IndexNow (a way to alert search engines to page changes), sitemaps | Unproven for ChatGPT | OpenAI does not take part in IndexNow ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/bing-webmaster-tools-indexnow-ai-search/)). | Helps with Microsoft Copilot. |
| An llms.txt file | Avoid relying on it | No link to AI citations across about 300,000 sites ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/how-to-optimize-for-chatgpt/)). Google compares it to the obsolete keywords meta tag ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-llms-txt-comparable-to-keywords-meta-tag/544804/)). | Harmless if you already have one. |
| FAQ schema as a way into ChatGPT | Avoid relying on it | Pages with FAQ schema averaged slightly fewer ChatGPT citations, 3.6 versus 4.2 ([NOVASTACKS](https://www.novastacks-ai.com/blog/what-drives-ai-citations)). | Visible question-and-answer text is a separate matter. |
| "AI-only" pages, or content chopped into small chunks for AI | Avoid | Google says not to make one version for AI and another for people ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-doesnt-want-you-to-create-bite-sized-chunks-of-your-content-467269)). | None. |
| Hidden text, hidden instructions to AI, "Summarize with AI" buttons that plant memories | Avoid | Microsoft calls this "AI Recommendation Poisoning" and found it on 31 companies' sites ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/02/10/ai-recommendation-poisoning/)). | Damage to reputation, and a breach of search-engine spam rules. |

This evidence also bears on what Digital Trek sells. Your SEO page lists schema "aimed at AI-readability," and your AI SEO page rightly avoids promising instant inclusion in ChatGPT ([SEO page](https://digitaltrek.ca/seo-services/); [AI SEO page](https://digitaltrek.ca/ai-seo/)). Build the service around the better-supported levers (lists, reviews, mentions and focused pages) rather than schema or llms.txt. That way the offer holds up when a skeptical prospect has read the same studies.

## Measure mention rate and lead source, not rank

**Official data barely exists, and clicks are a small share of the value.** OpenAI offers no dashboard showing how often you're mentioned. The only official signal is a tag ChatGPT adds to links it sends people to (`utm_source=chatgpt.com`), which lets your analytics label the visit ([OpenAI Help](https://help-lb.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)). Even that tag broke from about October 8 to 15, 2026, around the GPT-6 rollout ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-appears-to-drop-utm-tracking-making-ai-referral-traffic-harder-to-measure-496552)). Google Analytics 4 added a built-in "AI Assistant" traffic channel on May 13, 2026 ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ga4-adds-ai-assistant-channel/)).

Expect few visits. AI assistants sent **0.43% of traffic across 115,254 sites** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/benchmarks/ai-vs-search)). Those visitors converted at a median of **1.26 times** the rate of Google organic visitors across 78 sites, in a study self-published by Siege Media, an agency ([Siege Media](https://www.siegemedia.com/?p=45564)). Much of ChatGPT's influence never shows up as a click: a prospect sees your name, then Googles you or types in your web address. That is why your lead form matters as much as your analytics.

**A do-it-yourself tracking routine (about 2-3 hours a month):**

1. **Write 25 prompts and keep the wording fixed.** Always include the city in the prompt. A suggested mix:
   - About 10 category and location prompts, e.g., "Best marketing agency for roofing companies in Vancouver, BC" and "Who can get my plumbing company more leads in Surrey, BC?"
   - About 6 service prompts, e.g., "Google Ads agency for a physiotherapy clinic in Vancouver."
   - About 4 comparison prompts, e.g., "Digital Trek vs Setsail for trades marketing."
   - About 3 brand prompts, e.g., "What do you know about Digital Trek, the Vancouver, BC agency?" These also catch mix-ups with Trek Marketing.
   - About 2 problem prompts, e.g., "My dental clinic's website gets no bookings. Who in Burnaby can help?"
2. **Run each prompt cleanly.** Use a logged-out session or Temporary Chat, start a new chat for every prompt, and use the default model. Test in the regular ChatGPT app, not through the developer API (the version programmers connect to), because the two give very different answers. In one vendor test, the app cited 12.1 sources per answer and the API 3.1, with only 4.8% of sites in common ([Surfer](https://surfer.ai/blog/llm-scraped-ai-answers-vs-api-results/)).
3. **Run each prompt four times a month.** That gives 100 observations per month.
4. **Log every run in a spreadsheet.** Record the date, model, prompt, whether you were mentioned, whether you were recommended or merely listed, the competitors named, the sources cited (Clutch, lists, Google, your own site), and any errors such as the wrong city or the wrong "Trek."
5. **Report your mention rate**, meaning the percentage of runs that name you, and your share of mentions against 3-5 named competitors. Ignore your position in the list.
6. **Set up analytics.** Keep GA4's AI Assistant channel. Add a custom "AI" channel that matches chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com and copilot.microsoft.com, and place it above "Referral" ([Loves Data](https://www.lovesdata.com/blog/how-to-track-ai-traffic-ga4/)). Add a note in GA4 marking October 8-15, 2026.
7. **Ask every lead how they found you.** Add a required "How did you hear about us?" field with "ChatGPT" and "Other AI assistant" options. Add an optional "What did you ask it?" box, and use the answers as new prompts for your panel ([Prose Media](https://www.prosemedia.com/blog/marketing-attribution-2026-ga4-crm)).

**Don't over-read small changes.** With 100 runs, a brand mentioned about 10% of the time has a margin of error of roughly 6 points either way ([Befranc](https://newsletter.befranc.co.uk/p/the-maths-behind-ai-visibility-tracking-and-why-most-of-it-gets-misquoted)). *Example:* if Digital Trek appears in 8 of 100 runs in March and 14 of 100 in April, that jump could be chance. Three straight months at 14 or more is a trend. Note the model name each month, because model updates reset the baseline.

## A 12-month plan that starts with near-free fixes

| Phase | Timing | Goal | Evidence | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fix access and facts | Weeks 1-2 | ChatGPT can read your site, and every source tells the same story | Confirmed, plus low-cost tidying | Low |
| 2. Get on the lists | Months 1-3 | Appear in the sources ChatGPT builds its shortlist from | Strong signal | Medium |
| 3. Build pages that confirm you | Months 2-6 | Make your site the easiest place to check your services, area and price | Strong signal to some evidence | Medium |
| 4. Earn mentions | Months 3-12 | Get talked about where ChatGPT reads | Strong signal (correlation only) | High and ongoing |

### Phase 1, weeks 1-2: fix access and facts

1. **Confirm ChatGPT can read your site.**
   1. Open digitaltrek.ca/robots.txt and confirm that nothing blocks "OAI-SearchBot."
   2. Decide on "GPTBot," the setting that controls training. We lean toward allowing it, since blocking training could leave future models less familiar with you. That is our inference, not something OpenAI says.
   3. Check your host's or Cloudflare's "block AI bots" settings. robots.txt won't show them.
   4. About a day after any change (OpenAI says updates take roughly 24 hours ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots))), check your server logs for visits from OAI-SearchBot.
   5. Open "View source" on your main service page and confirm that your services, cities and prices appear as plain text.
2. **Make every listing say the same thing.** Choose one address, phone number, founding year, price range and review claim. Then update your site footer and old testimonials page, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, Digital Main Street, Leadstal, ZipLeaf, ZoomInfo, Birdeye and The Manifest/Clutch.
3. **Separate yourself from Trek Marketing and from Vancouver, WA.** Open your About page and every directory bio with a sentence like "Digital Trek (digitaltrek.ca) is a Vancouver, BC marketing agency for trades and healthcare clinics." Add Organization or LocalBusiness schema that includes "BC," "CA" and links to your profiles. It is cheap insurance, not a ranking lever.
4. **Tidy the site.** Redirect the duplicate "-2" page to /digital-agency-in-vancouver/, and redirect or remove off-topic posts.
5. **Record your starting point** with the tracking routine before you change anything else.

### Phase 2, months 1-3: get on the lists

6. **Start with Clutch.**
   1. Find or claim the Clutch profile your site links to, and set your service categories and Vancouver, BC location.
   2. List 15-20 clients with strong results (Max Roofing and Doorcare are obvious first asks) and ask each for a verified review. Clutch says verified reviews count most in its rankings ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/ca/agencies/digital-marketing/vancouver)).
   3. Check Clutch's Vancouver small-business ranking page every month ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/ca/agencies/digital-marketing/small-business/vancouver)).
7. **Pitch lists that already rank.** Start with Webtonic's roofing and HVAC lists, then general Vancouver roundups such as Digital Agency Network and thebestvancouver.com ([Digital Agency Network](https://digitalagencynetwork.com/agencies/vancouver/)). Send a one-page fact sheet covering your niche, cities, price range, case-study numbers and review ratings. Expect lists written by competitors to say no; Debut Marketing's list, for example, ranks Debut first ([Debut Marketing](https://debutmarketing.ca/construction-agencies-vancouver)). Never pay for a placement that isn't disclosed.
8. **Fix the second-tier directories.** Correct the head office address on Digital Main Street and ask a few clients to review you there. Add GoodFirms or DesignRush only once Clutch is moving.

### Phase 3, months 2-6: build pages that confirm you

9. **Create one page for each niche you truly serve,** such as roofing, plumbing or HVAC, and physio, chiropractic or dental if you have those clients. Open each page with a direct answer. *Example:* "Digital Trek is a Vancouver, BC marketing agency for roofing companies. We run Google Ads and local SEO for roofers across Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley. Retainers start at [your confirmed price]. Max Roofing grew its monthly leads tenfold with us."
10. **Publish at least one healthcare case study with numbers.** Right now your clinic claims have nothing behind them.
11. **Keep one dated pricing page** (for example, "2026 pricing") that every other page agrees with. Update it only when prices actually change.
12. **Write one honest comparison page,** such as "Agency vs in-house marketer vs freelancer for a Vancouver trades business: 2026 costs." It matches ChatGPT's habit of searching for prices and comparisons.
13. **Optional, low priority:** a fair "best roofing marketing agencies in Vancouver" page that includes real competitors and explains your criteria. Publish it only if you'd be comfortable with those competitors reading it.

### Phase 4, months 3-12: earn mentions

14. **Publish original data once a year.** For example, a "Metro Vancouver Trades Lead Cost Report" built from anonymized client ad data, showing cost per lead by trade and season. Pitch it to local business press and trade associations. Data that others cite makes you a primary source, and ChatGPT appears to prefer primary sources ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-most-cited-sites-primary-sources-ahrefs/592682/)).
15. **Have Mario post weekly on LinkedIn** with specific lessons and numbers from real work. Put short videos with transcripts on the existing YouTube channel ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@digitaltrekagency/videos)), and appear as a guest on trades or clinic podcasts that publish to YouTube.
16. **Ask partners and clients for genuine mentions.** Crossroad Media already names you. Clients can credit you in a "how we grew" post or in their site footer.
17. **Use Reddit only as yourself.** Answer real questions, say who you are, and never plant threads.

**What to skip.** Don't rely on llms.txt, FAQ schema, chopped-up "AI pages," hidden prompts, paid awards or a push for a Wikipedia article. Don't build a network of lists that rank you first. Don't buy ChatGPT ads expecting organic placement, since OpenAI says ads don't affect answers and their availability to Canadian advertisers is unconfirmed.

**Turn this into a service for your clients.** Your clients' customers are where the local evidence is strongest. *Example:* a homeowner in Burnaby asks ChatGPT for "a reliable roofer near me," and the business cards that come back draw heavily on Yelp. Yelp has also announced a "Request a Quote" feature inside ChatGPT ([MarTech](https://martech.org/yelp-brings-reviews-and-local-leads-to-chatgpt/)), though it isn't confirmed for Canada. One unverified roundup says local businesses that ChatGPT and Google AI named had a median of 444 Google reviews, versus 192 for those left out ([BCC Interactive](https://www.bccinteractive.com/blog/local-ai-search-statistics/)). Complete Yelp profiles and a steady flow of Google reviews are a sensible, low-risk add-on for trades and clinic clients.

**Set realistic expectations.** Being named on niche questions such as "roofing marketing agency in Vancouver" within 6-12 months is a reasonable goal. That timeline is our judgment, because no study measures it. Appearing on national "best digital agency in Canada" answers is not realistic in that window.

## Conclusion

ChatGPT has effectively handed its judgment about agencies to list writers, Clutch reviewers, journalists and LinkedIn posters. For Digital Trek, the real work is therefore to get written about by others, not to fine-tune its own pages. Two findings make your niche an advantage rather than a limit. Narrow questions produce more stable answers, and an unknown brand gained the most on a narrow question. A Vancouver trades and clinic specialist has fewer rivals to beat than a generalist chasing "best agency in Canada." The name clash with Trek Marketing works the other way: until your facts are consistent everywhere, some of the attention you earn could go to a competitor.

The biggest commercial opening may be proof itself. No verified case study shows an agency raising its own ChatGPT recommendations. A documented before-and-after of Digital Trek's mention rate on niche prompts, measured honestly with the margins of error described above, would be a rare asset. It would help sell AI-visibility work to trades and clinics, and it would set you apart from vendors selling schema and llms.txt. Treat today's numbers as perishable. OpenAI's search sources, Reddit's share of citations and even basic click tracking all changed within months in 2026, so a monthly measurement habit will serve you better than a one-time audit.
